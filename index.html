What we check

Nine things your PnL will not tell you.

Every check exists to catch a habit that looks fine on a good week and empties an account on a bad one. Your total profit is not one of them.

⌁

Risk-adjusted return

Return per unit of volatility over a wallet’s whole history. Rejects 300% made by running maximum leverage into a trend that happened to hold.

⇄

Position sizing

Whether size answers conviction or the last result. Rejects martingale — doubling into losers until one comes back.

◉

Drawdown control

Depth and duration of losing periods, not the single worst day. Rejects a record that spends a year underwater and reports only its maximum drawdown.

⊞

Concentration

How much of lifetime profit came from the largest few positions. Rejects one lucky trade carrying an otherwise flat record.

∞

Fee & funding drag

Realised cost as a share of gross profit, funding included. Rejects an edge that is real gross and gone net.

⌖

Strategy stability

Whether a wallet still trades the way it used to. Rejects a trader whose edge was real in one regime and who has quietly become someone else.

◈

Distance to liquidation

How close positions were allowed to run to being wiped out. Rejects a record that survived by two percent and calls it risk control.

◐

Winners vs losers held

Whether losers are held longer than winners. Rejects the disposition effect, which flatters a win rate while draining the account.

↻

Recovery after a loss

What leverage does in the days following a large loss. Rejects revenge trading, which shows up in the data long before it shows up in your balance.

Specification

Nine questions, asked of the data.

Pick any wallet. Each check below is computed from that wallet’s own trades, drawn so you can see what it found, and explained without the algebra. The formulas are one click away if you want them.

Composite and calibration

Each component is mapped to its cross-sectional percentile within the eligible universe, so no absolute cut-off is invented: S = 100 · Σ wⱼ · rankⱼ.

The weights wⱼ are not chosen by hand. They are fitted out of sample and only on data held back from every allocation decision. Until that fit is complete the composite runs equal-weighted, and it is reported as equal-weighted.

Formulas make the method falsifiable and reproducible. They do not make it correct. Whether these components predict how a wallet performs under allocated capital is settled by out-of-sample results against a control, not by the algebra above.
